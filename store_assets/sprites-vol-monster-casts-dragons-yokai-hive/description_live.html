Bestiary is 12 top-down pixel art monster sprites for Godot 4, RPG Maker MZ and any engine that reads PNG sheets: dragons, yokai and giant bugs. Use them in commercial and free games, no credit needed.

What is inside

12 monsters · 5 animations each · 4 directions · sheets and single frames at 160 and 80 px

  • Dragons and drakes (4): ember drake that rests sitting, jade dragon with its wings spread, great blue dragon and storm raptor with an orange-red crest.
  • Oni and yokai (4): huge red oni, crow tengu with a long beak, squat river kappa with a white dish on its head and white kitsune with one great tail.
  • Giant bugs (4): mantis with two scythe forelegs, black spider, scorpion with its stinger raised and hornet on four pale wings.
  • Every monster has an idle, a walk (6 frames), a strike (6), a hurt (6) and a death (6) facing south, west, east and north: 1,200 frames at each size.
  • They fight with their own bodies: fists, jaws, scythes and a stinger. No weapons to lose, and no projectile sprites.
  • One 128 colour palette and one scale, so all 12 stand in one scene at 1:1, the kappa beside the dragon.

One frame from every state, every monster, at 1x of the 160 px master.

Every monster in all four facings, back views included.

Works with

Godot 4: 24 ready SpriteFrames files, checked in Godot 4.7.2. RPG Maker MZ: 24 walk sheets for img/characters. Any engine: sheets, single PNG frames and frames.json.

Good to know

Strikes, hurts and deaths are one-shots. One side view of each monster is the other mirrored; the kappa's front and back are each mirrored from one half. Three deaths end on a frame made from the idle, and the kappa and spider barely move in profile when they strike. Some parts change between views, such as the mantis scythes. The full Readme lists what each strike does and every small variation we found.

Licence and how it was made

Use the sprites in commercial and free games, no credit needed. Do not resell them as a sprite pack.

The sprites were generated with an AI image model (PixelLab), then palette locked and checked frame by frame by our tools. The packaging code was written with AI.
