CARILLON is live, free, and playable in your browser.

It is a tower defence whose towers never shoot. You hang bells; each one sounds a ring on the metronome; and where two rings touch, a spark is born. The spark is the only thing in the game that does damage. Nothing on the board has a range, a target, a cooldown or a projectile, because the entire weapon is the intersection of two circles.

The practical effect is that placement is aiming. Two bells that ring together have rings of the same size, so every place they can meet lies on the straight line halfway between them, and the game draws that line for you. Hang your bells so it crosses the road.

The part that took the longest to get right

The second system is phase. A damper walks the road carrying wet felt and knocks nearby bells off the beat, and a bell off the beat has its chords bow away from where you aimed them. So taking damage degrades your geometry rather than a hit point bar.

That design failed twice before it worked, and both failures are the same shape. Uniform drift is harmless. Two bells that are both a half beat late still have rings of equal size and a perfectly straight chord between them, so a damper that knocked the whole battery off the beat together changed nothing at all. Worse, in that state, tuning a single bell back onto the metronome is what creates the mismatch, and a battery that never tuned measurably outperformed one that did.

The fix was to give the damper distance falloff so it catches one bell of a pair and misses the other, and then to give every bell its own sour note so a heavily damped battery ends up scattered rather than uniformly late. Only the difference between two bells ever mattered, and it took a headless probe playing whole campaigns to see it.

What you get

  • Twelve waves, four bell metals, three interventions, and a persistent foundry.
  • A lost siege costs you the siege and never the foundry. You never start from nothing twice.
  • Autosave after every single action.
  • Mouse only. About half a megabyte. No account, no install.

The sound is the mechanic too: a bell is an inharmonic partial stack, so every bell sounds its own metal's note as it rings and the music you hear is the state of your battery. It is all procedural synthesis written by hand rather than a generative model.
