THE WARM ROOM is live, free, and plays in your browser.

You are given the key to a derelict public glasshouse at the edge of town in the first week of winter, and nobody asks for anything back. The glass is broken in places, the stove is cold, and there are ninety days until spring.

The line on the store page is a mechanic, not a mood

"The heat in the room is made of the people in it" is literally how the simulation works. Every visitor standing inside is a heat source in the same thermal field the stove writes into, and the fire runs to a thermostat, so a busy afternoon means the stove damps itself down and your wood lasts longer. In the game's own numbers: same building, same stove, same target temperature, and with twelve people in it the fire runs at 68% instead of 100% while the room is warmer anyway. That turns hospitality from a score into a fuel strategy.

Three systems that are really one problem

Heat is a per-cell field: it diffuses between floor cells, leaks through each individual pane according to how sound that pane is, comes in through the glass while the sun is up, and is banked by water barrels that soak up the afternoon and give it back after dark. That is why placement matters more than capacity. A stove against a wall spends half its output heating the outside.

Crops have temperature bands rather than timers, so there is a real ceiling on the obvious answer: above its ideal a plant does not grow any faster, and below its minimum it stops and starts taking cold damage. Corn salad and kale survive almost anything. Winter tomatoes and a lemon tree are a bet on how warm you can hold one corner of one room until March.

And people arrive because it is warmer in here than out there, which is why the hardest weeks are also the busiest. Each visitor wants one small thing, and meeting it is what turns a stranger into a regular and a regular into the glazier who mends a pane every other day.

Nothing here can be lost

There is no death and no restart in this game. If every plant dies you carry on in a colder room and build it back with what is left. It autosaves at the end of every day, in your browser only: no account, no server, no tracking, and no network calls of any kind once the page has loaded.

Made from nothing but code

Four tiling materials and 87 hand authored sprites, all produced by our own pixel engine, and every sound synthesised as you play. The source ships raw and readable in the build you are playing.

Free. Mouse only. About half an hour to reach spring.
