The Hearthhands village harbour, at work. 10 working people of the quay: three who keep the harbour (harbourmaster, lighthouse keeper and bosun), four who work the boats and the quay (dockhand, shipwright, ferryman and deckhand) and three who work the shore (net mender, salt raker and crabber). Every one of them has an idle, a 6-frame walk and a 6-frame job loop in all four directions, front, back and both profiles.

They do the trade, not just stand in it. Each one carries the tool of the trade, and the job loop is the trade itself: the spyglass goes to the eye, the mallet goes up, the brush scrubs, the rake pulls.

It drops straight into the Hearthhands village. Hearthhands 01: The Working Day, Hearthhands 02: Market and Craft, Hearthhands 03: Watch and Ward, Hearthhands Hallowtide: the Costumed Village and Hearthhands 04: Hearth and Kin hold 49 villagers. This pack shares their 200 and 100 cells and their ground line (every foot on row 168 of the 200 cell, measured across all 59 villagers of the six packs), and the palette holds every one of the series' 43 colours unchanged, plus 53 more fitted to this cast.

The moves

  • Harbourmaster: raises his brass spyglass to his eye in both hands, looks out to sea, and lowers it.
  • Dockhand: hoists the rope coil off his shoulder to head height and settles it back (side and back views); facing you he tugs his cap down with both hands.
  • Net mender: brings the net bundle up to her chest in both hands and works it; in the east profile she stretches the mesh open between her hands.
  • Shipwright: raises the long mallet overhead (front and back views) or swings it up onto his shoulder (side views) and brings it down again.
  • Ferryman: swings the oar up from his side to lie level across his body in both hands, and lowers it.
  • Deckhand: bends to scrub at his boots with the deck brush in both hands.
  • Lighthouse keeper: lifts the lit storm lantern out to chest height in profile (waist height facing you and from behind) and lowers it.
  • Bosun: cups one hand to his mouth to call an order and signals with the other arm high, then lowers both.
  • Salt raker: draws the wooden salt rake in along the ground toward her feet and pushes it out again.
  • Crabber: lifts the wicker crab pot up to look into it and lowers it again.

The consistency proof

The honest worry about generated art is that frame 40 will not match frame 1. This pack proves it against the decoded pixels of every file in the download:

  • 1,180 files are real PNGs and all 1,180 decode
  • 6,577,911 opaque pixels, every one inside the declared 96-colour palette
  • 84,000,000 pixels carry strictly binary alpha, no anti-aliasing, no soft upscale
  • 2,080 frame-map checks: every one of the 520 frames per grid is declared, lands inside its sheet and has a real file at both grids
  • every asset present at one grid size is present at the other

Honest notes

  • The EAST idle is the generated WEST idle mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: dockhand, harbourmaster, saltRaker, shipwright.
  • The WEST idle is the generated EAST idle mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: crabber, deckhand, ferryman, lighthouseKeeper.
  • The EAST job is the generated WEST job mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: dockhand, harbourmaster, saltRaker, shipwright.
  • The WEST job is the generated EAST job mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: crabber, deckhand, ferryman, lighthouseKeeper.
  • The EAST walk is the generated WEST walk mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: dockhand, harbourmaster, saltRaker, shipwright.
  • The WEST walk is the generated EAST walk mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: crabber, deckhand, ferryman, lighthouseKeeper.
  • Every job cycle plays its motion out and back (up, then the same frames down again), so the loop closes exactly on the standing pose.
  • Eight of the ten ship ONE side view (idle, walk and job together) as an exact mirror of the other side. For most it is the tool: carried in one hand, it sat on the far side of the body in that view and the generated art hid it there (the oar, the deck brush, the lantern, the crab pot and the mallet in the walk; the salt rake already in her side view). For the harbourmaster the generated east walk drew a satchel he does not wear, and for the dockhand the generated east job floated the rope coil off his shoulder with no hand on it. Mirroring keeps the tool in view in every frame and keeps idle, walk and job consistent in that facing; the tool moves to the other hand there. The exact list of mirrored cycles is given just above these notes.
  • The salt raker's side-view job kicks up a small puff of salt at the rake head as she pulls it in; it is drawn into the art, not a separate effect.
  • The dockhand's job reads differently facing you: from the front he tugs his cap down with both hands; in profile and from behind he hoists the rope coil off his shoulder.
  • Walking toward you, the ferryman and the net-mender take short careful steps (his boots shift, her skirt hides the stride); their full stride shows in the side views.
  • The salt raker's rake carries a small white lump of salt at its head in every frame; it is part of the tool, not an effect.
