He is not lying to you. He is legislating.

A narrator describes the room you are standing in. His sentences lie on the floor where he said them, and they are not true yet.

Walk at a door he called locked and you go straight through it, because you never accepted that it was. Stop and take his word for it and the door becomes genuinely, mechanically locked, and you have lost that way out for good.

Every sentence is a two-sided tool. "The east wall is only painted" is a way through if you accept it. "The reading room is watching" is how you die if you do.

The four things worth knowing before you start

Taking his word for it is free. Refusing him is rationed. Each chapter gives you a few doubts. Spend them on the sentences that would hurt you if he finished them, not on the ones that are merely inconvenient.

Every refusal makes him bolder, and so does every agreement. A narrator who is agreed with does not get quieter. Past a threshold he stops waiting to be believed: a red line crawls under each sentence you left alone, and when it reaches the end the sentence is true and the room has changed around you.

Two things that cannot both be so will stop the room working. He will tell you the floor over the gap holds. He will also tell you there is no floor over the gap. Accept both and the room turns against itself and will not let you leave until you go back and refuse one. That is not a bug, and the game says so at the time.

Losing costs you a chapter, never the reading. You keep every liberty you have learned to take with his text, and every trap you now know about. There is no way to lose the whole run.

Controls

Keyboard. Arrows or WASD to walk. E take his word for it, Q doubt it, Space choose between two sentences you can reach at once. Later chapters add F pin, Shift+Q cut off a whole paragraph, C carry a sentence and say it at something else, and X unsay one entirely. H for how he works.

Phone or tablet. Tap the floor to walk, tap a sentence within reach to choose it, then tap one of the verbs along the bottom. Every verb is a real button, and the whole game is finishable without a keyboard.

What it is made of

Five chapters, fifteen rooms, thirty-four sentences. No framework, no build step, no minification, and no font file: the alphabet is fifty-one glyphs of stroke geometry drawn at runtime, which is the only way a sentence can be a hollow outline when it is untested, solid ink when you accept it, and struck through when you refuse it. The stone, the water and the wood come from our own pixel engine. The eleven sound cues are arithmetic.

It saves as you go, so you can close the tab and come back. It is free, and it always will be.
