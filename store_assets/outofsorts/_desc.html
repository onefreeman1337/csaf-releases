A typing roguelite set in a letterpress print shop.

Words crawl down the galley towards your composing stick. Type one and you set it, and the trouble it was carrying goes away.

But you do not have a keyboard. You have a TYPE CASE. 12 E. 9 T. 3 U. One Q. One Z. Every letter you type is a piece of metal lifted out of that drawer, and it does not come back until you break the forme up.

So the skill is not speed. It is deciding what to spend. Set three long words full of E and the short easy ones in front of you turn grey, because the shop cannot set them any more.

Out of sorts is a printer's phrase four hundred years older than the mood it now describes. It means the compositor has run out of a letter and cannot set the line. In this game it is how you lose.

What makes it different

Every typing game ever made treats the alphabet as free and infinite: the scarce things are TIME and ACCURACY. Here the alphabet is the inventory, so the scarce thing is the letter E. Your vocabulary does not shrink randomly — it shrinks in a shape you chose.

  • The case is the board. A word you cannot afford cannot be typed at all, and the slip tells you exactly which sorts you are short of. The drawer at the bottom of the screen shows every bin, as wide as it is deep, emptying as you work.
  • A mistake is metal on the floor. A mistyped letter goes to the hell box, where distribution cannot reach it. Accuracy costs you sorts, not points.
  • SPACE distributes. Two and a bit seconds with your hands off the keyboard, the whole forme back in its boxes, and the galley does not stop coming. Distributing early is cheap. Distributing when you are already stuck is not.
  • Five kinds of trouble, and each one is a real printing fault. A monk is over-inked and costs twice the metal. A devil robs your case instead of spoiling the sheet. A quad is a blank slug that takes two words to shift. An orphan is short and fast.
  • 10 formes to print, then the night shift, which does not end.

The foundry

Between formes you spend impressions on things that change which words are affordable — never on a damage number, because there are no damage numbers in this game. Cast more of a letter you keep running out of (the offers follow what you actually spent tonight). Buy a ligature so TH costs one sort instead of two. Take an alloy that hands the rarest sort of a clean line straight back. A spoiled page costs the page: you keep half the impressions and every single thing in the foundry, and you set that page again with a deeper case than you had before.

Everything here was made for it

  • No font ships, and none was used. The face is a stroke skeleton — stems, bowls and diagonals in em units — that the game gives bracketed serifs, stroke contrast and vertical stress at render time, then casts twice: once as a piece of metal type with a body exactly as wide as the letter's own advance and the nick groove a compositor feels for in the dark, once as a printed impression with ink squash and the bite of the shoulder into damp paper. 74 letterforms, 158 distinct generated pieces of art including 10 printer's ornaments, out of about ninety lines of geometry.
  • 1125 hand-chosen words in five bands, including the shop's own trade vocabulary — quoin, galley, hellbox, dissing, catchword. No dictionary was downloaded. 8 of them cannot be set at all on a stock case, so they simply do not appear until the foundry has cast the metal for them.
  • Every sound is a thing happening to metal, synthesised from the modes of a small metal bar, and the music is played on the same voice the sorts click with.

How to play

A–Z sets the word you are typing — nothing is spent until it is finished. SPACE distributes the forme. BACKSPACE drops the line. ESC brings up the rules at any time. Keyboard only. The game saves after every action, so closing the tab costs you nothing.

Free, and it plays in the browser.
