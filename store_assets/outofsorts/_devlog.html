OUT OF SORTS is live, free, and plays in your browser.

It is a typing roguelite set in a letterpress print shop, and the thing it does that no other typing game does is take the alphabet away from you. You do not have a keyboard. You have a type case: a drawer holding a finite count of every letter, in the proportions a real compositor's case was laid in. Twelve E. Nine T. Three U. One Q. One Z.

Every character you type is a piece of metal lifted out of that drawer, and it does not come back until you break the forme up. So the skill is not speed. It is deciding what to spend.

Why that one change moves everything

Typing games are universally built on the assumption that letters are free and infinite: the scarce resources are time and accuracy. Make the letters scarce and the genre reorganises itself. A long word full of common letters is a heavy hit you may not be able to afford twice. Your vocabulary does not shrink randomly as the night goes on, it shrinks in a shape you chose three words ago. And the fail state is not death — it is the phrase the game is named after. A compositor who has run out of a letter cannot set the line. That is a printer's phrase four hundred years older than the mood it now describes, and it is how you lose.

The two systems feed each other rather than sitting side by side. Setting words spends the case; the case decides which words are settable; and the foundry between formes buys things that change which words are affordable rather than a damage number. There are no damage numbers in this game. Cast more of a letter you keep running out of, and the offers follow what you actually spent tonight. Buy a ligature so TH costs one sort instead of two. Take an alloy that hands the rarest sort of a clean line straight back to the drawer.

Nothing here is a font

No typeface ships with this game and none was used to make it. The face is a stroke skeleton — stems, bowls and diagonals in em units — that the game gives bracketed serifs, stroke contrast and vertical stress at render time, and then casts twice: once as a piece of metal type, with a body exactly as wide as that letter's own advance and the nick groove a compositor feels for in the dark; once as a printed impression, with ink squash and the bite of the shoulder into damp paper. Seventy four letterforms, rendered both ways, plus ten printer's ornaments: 158 distinct generated pieces of art out of about ninety lines of geometry.

Every sound is a thing happening to metal, synthesised from the modes of a small metal bar, and the shop's music is played on the same voice the sorts click with. The 1,125 words were chosen by hand in five bands, including the trade's own vocabulary — quoin, galley, hellbox, dissing, catchword. Eight of them cannot be set at all on a stock case, so they do not appear until the foundry has cast the metal for them.

The defect that only showed up when the menu was looked at

The foundry is where the whole design lives, and for most of the build it was a plain text list of nine offers. Every mechanical check passed over it. It was fixed by giving each offer a mark drawn by the game's own renderer at that offer's real data — a cast offer draws the actual metal sort you are buying, a ligature is set through the game's own compositor so two abutted bodies read as the one wider body they really are, and each upgrade draws an ornament.

The first attempt at that was a regression, and nothing caught it either: at the size it was drawn the marks pushed a card under the sticky buttons and wrapped three descriptions onto a second line. It was found by opening the screenshot. The guard that now exists reads every mark back off the bitmap and requires that the nine marks differ from each other — a mark that always drew the letter "a" would pass every pixel test while telling you nothing.

Losing costs the round, never the shop

A spoiled page costs the page. You keep half the impressions and every single thing you have cast, and you set that page again with a deeper case than you had before. The game saves after every action, so closing the tab costs you nothing.

Keyboard only. A–Z sets, SPACE distributes the forme, BACKSPACE drops the line, ESC brings up the rules at any time.
