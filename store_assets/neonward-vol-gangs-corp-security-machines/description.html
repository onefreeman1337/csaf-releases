Nine top-down cyberpunk pixel art enemies for Godot 4, RPG Maker MZ or any engine that reads PNG sprite sheets. Three casts in one neon-and-grime style: two street gangers, three corporate security troopers and four machines (an android, a two-legged mech, a hover drone and a spider drone). Each ships idle, walk, attack, hurt and death in four directions, 900 frames at 160x160 plus the same 900 at 80x80. Six fight in melee and three shoot. Commercial use allowed, no credit required.

The enemy roster for Neonward 01: Street Cast. Same palette (all of its colours, unchanged) and the same human build, so these gangers and troopers belong on the same street as its netrunner, courier, street samurai and street kid.

Every character fights: 100 frames each, a 6-frame walk, a 6-frame attack, a 6-frame hurt and a 6-frame death in all four directions. The 160x160 frames are the native master (nothing upscaled); the 80x80 set is an exact 2:1 reduction, palette-locked so it stays crisp.

The attacks

  • Brawler (a punk brawler in spiked knuckle gauntlets; melee): throws a straight knuckle-gauntlet punch (side views), lifts both fists and brings them down to cross over the chest (front), or raises both fists overhead and drops back to rest (back).
  • Combat android (a silver combat android with amber joints; melee): jabs an arm out from a fighting stance (front), throws a short forward punch (side views), or raises its guard (back).
  • Gang boss (the gang boss, with a chrome arm; melee): punches with the near hand (side views), thrusts the chrome arm out to his side from a fighting stance, turning side-on (front), or swings a long black baton up over his shoulder and down (back).
  • Heavy gunner (a corp heavy gunner with a big machine gun; gunfire): brings the big gun up and fires it with a star-shaped muzzle flash.
  • Hover drone (a hovering security drone with a red lens; gunfire): fires a bright blast from its lens, drawn with a burst of sparks and a ring of particles (front and side views); from behind, where the lens faces away, the shot shows as a salmon-red flash through the whole shell.
  • Riot trooper (a corp riot trooper with a tall shield; melee): punches past the raised shield (west), drives the shield forward in a bash (east), or raises a fist and hammers it down (front and back).
  • Security mech (a heavy two-legged security mech; melee): swings both clawed arms wide and smashes them down across its front (front and back) or drives both fists forward (side views).
  • SMG guard (a corp security guard with a compact SMG; gunfire): fires the compact submachine gun from the hip in a burst of muzzle flashes.
  • Spider drone (a four-legged spider drone; melee): rears up and splays its legs wide (front and back) or reaches its legs out and tilts forward (side views).

Every hurt flinches and returns to the standing pose; every death ends on the ground (attack, hurt, death are one-shots).

The consistency proof

The honest worry about generated art is that frame 40 will not match frame 1. This pack proves it against the decoded pixels of every file in the download:

  • 1,998 files are real PNGs and all 1,998 decode
  • 11,198,454 opaque pixels, every one inside the declared 128-colour palette
  • 89,856,000 pixels carry strictly binary alpha, no anti-aliasing, no soft upscale
  • 3,600 frame-map checks: all 900 entries land inside their sheets and every frame has a real file at both sizes
  • every asset present at one grid size is present at the other

What you get

  • 900 frames at 160x160 (native) and 900 at 80x80 (an exact 2:1 reduction)
  • 45 sheets per grid, one per character per state, plus every frame as its own PNG
  • frames.json with every frame's sheet, x, y, w, h, and pack.json listing the states and which are one-shots
  • RPG Maker MZ: 18 $-prefixed 3x4 walk sheets (both grids), drop into img/characters
  • Godot 4: a SpriteFrames .tres per character per grid, every animation named and sliced, attack, hurt, death set to play once, loaded and verified in Godot 4.7.2 before shipping
  • the palette holds all 64 colours of the Neonward 01 palette, plus 64 fitted to this cast

Honest notes

  • The EAST attack is the generated WEST attack mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: brawler.
  • The WEST attack is the generated EAST attack mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: gangBoss, heavyGunner, smgGuard, spiderDrone.
  • The WEST death is the generated EAST death mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: spiderDrone.
  • The WEST hurt is the generated EAST hurt mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: spiderDrone.
  • The WEST idle is the generated EAST idle mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: spiderDrone.
  • The WEST walk is the generated EAST walk mirrored, pixel for pixel, for: spiderDrone.
  • Attack, hurt and death are one-shots: every attack plays out once, every hurt closes back on the standing pose, and every death ends lying on the ground.
  • Three characters attack with guns (the SMG guard, the heavy gunner and the hover drone). Their muzzle flashes and the drone's lens blast are drawn into the attack frames; there are no separate projectile sprites.
  • Seen head-on and from behind, the SMG guard and the heavy gunner aim and fire toward screen-right rather than straight at or away from the camera; their side views fire along the facing.
  • The hover drone floats above the ground line the walkers stand on. Its walk is a hover loop in place, and when it dies it rolls or flips over and drops to that ground line, throwing a few orange sparks as it falls west and showing a cyan glint as it falls east; in its north death the rotors turn to spiky blades.
  • Three back views (north) are weaker than their other facings: the brawler raises both fists overhead, where they flash white, and drops back to rest without a visible blow; the combat android raises its guard, with small white glints at its hands; and the hover drone shows its shot as a flash through its shell because its lens faces away.
  • The riot trooper's front attack paints a large white impact burst at hip level over his body and the shield edge as the blow lands.
  • In profile the hover drone's rotor arms sit hidden behind its round body, so its side views show the shell, the lens and the antenna (a double antenna facing east, a single one facing west), and a small cream-orange glow sits on its front during the east hover.
  • Some hurts are a small flinch rather than a big recoil: the SMG guard's (all four facings), the gang boss's east and back views, the riot trooper's back view, and the heavy gunner's back view, where mainly the helmet lifts.
  • From behind, the heavy gunner's walk hides the gun in front of his body (only a pale block of the grip shows) and shows white elbow pads; in his front hurt the helmet lifts off his eyes for two frames, and in his west hurt a black faceplate covers his face for two frames.
  • In the gang boss's back-view attack his chrome arm is out of sight for two frames while the baton crosses his shoulders.
  • Small variations a sharp eye will find: the riot trooper's face reads ruddy and hairless head-on but pale with dark hair in profile; his shield bends as he falls south and is hidden under him in the last frame of his north death; the security mech's visor flashes hot pink in one frame of its front hurt, its side hurts twist the shoulder toward the camera, and its forearms fold in over its thighs at the end of its front attack; the combat android's hands blur over its middle in one frame of its front hurt; the SMG guard's near hand shows bare skin for two frames of his east walk; and the spider drone's east hurt (and so its mirrored west hurt) grows a thin antenna for three frames.
  • The mirrored cycles listed above swap sides, so the gang boss's chrome arm and the gunners' guns change hands between their east and west attacks. The spider drone's whole west facing is its east facing mirrored, because the generated west lost the drone's lit eye.
