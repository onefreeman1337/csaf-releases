BITTERWORKS is live and free in the browser. A soda works on a drying salt lake: sink wells into the brine under the bed, run it east through evaporation pans, crystallise salt and bittern, still the bittern into potash, and burn the two back together into soda ash for the railhead.

What is actually new here

Most games of this shape put you on a static map where the answer to every bottleneck is more space. Here space is fixed and the GROUND is the thing that changes. Brine is a subsurface field, per cell. Pumping takes it out faster than the lake puts it back, and a small share of what you take never comes back at all, because the ground itself is used up. Recharge comes from the lake and falls away sharply with distance from the water, and at the start of years 3, 5 and 7 the water recedes another column west and uncovers new bed.

So the best ground on the lake is always the ground the water has just left, and wells are meant to be moved. That is measurable rather than a claim: the game ships a campaign probe that plays four whole eight year leases with no graphics, and a works that follows the water west shipped 154% of the standing order where an identical works that never relocates a well shipped 122%.

There are no belts

A machine feeds only the four cells it touches. A cistern holds one material and passes it along, which is the only way across a gap, and it costs a whole cell to cross one. Pans run as a cascade. A crystalliser with nowhere to put its bittern tips it back into the bed, like every other works on this lake, and the ledger counts every tonne. A still is what makes this one different.

Made of

Hand written HTML5, one canvas, no engine and no framework. Every plate and sprite is generated by our own pixel tools and the music was composed as a score and rendered by our own oscillator synth. No stock art, no asset packs, no image model, no sample library. Code, graphics and writing are AI written; the sounds are procedural synthesis, which is not generative audio, and the listing says exactly that.
