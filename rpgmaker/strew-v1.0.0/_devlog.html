Strew is out: a plugin that dresses the RPG Maker MZ maps you made by hand, from your own tileset, in one keypress.

Most hand-made maps are one grass tile repeated, a road, a pond and a few trees. Filling them with flowers, stones and bushes square by square is the slowest part of mapping, and scattering things at random makes a map look noisy rather than lived in. Strew places by rules instead: a slow field decides where flowers gather and which flower each drift is, rocks and bushes settle against trees, walls and water, and a field of one repeated floor tile is broken up by its own variants.

What we measured while building it

  • On a 60 by 60 meadow, Strew's flowers are about nine times more clustered than the same number dropped at random (quadrat dispersion 6.85 against 0.76, averaged over 20 seeds), and 86% of neighbouring flowers are the same kind.
  • On RPG Maker's stock Outside sheet, reading tiles by colour alone would have scattered pots, a snowman and skeletons. Strew uses a palette picked by hand there, and reads the art only on other tilesets.
  • One stock flower is a single small bloom in the middle of its square; packed into a drift it stamped a grid. Strew now measures how much of its square each flower spans and keeps single blooms apart.
  • Before placing anything that blocks, Strew proves the squares around it stay connected. Across 200 seeds on the demo map the number of separate walkable areas never changed.

Bake or Live

F7 in playtest bakes the dressing into the map file and keeps the previous file; F7 again rerolls; Shift+F7 undoes exactly what Strew placed. Or tag a map <Strew Live> and it is dressed in memory as it loads, the same way every time, with no file written.
