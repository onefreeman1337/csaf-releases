Temperament: personality quizzes that sort the player into your own classes, heroes or houses

Some of the best RPG openings never ask the player to pick from a menu. They ask a few questions, read the answers, and tell you who you are. Temperament brings that to RPG Maker MZ: the player answers situations where no answer is right, and is sorted into one of your outcomes. That can be a class from your database, one of your actors (a "who are you?" opening that picks the starting hero), or houses you name, for an academy sorting or a fortune teller in a tent.

The quiz draws from 44 built-in situations with 176 answers, plus any you write in the plugin parameters. Each answer leans toward one or two of twelve temperaments, and each quiz is drawn so that every temperament your outcomes care about gets offered. When the last answer is in, a twelve-spoke wheel fills from the answers, the winning spoke flares, and a card turns over with the outcome's portrait or crest, its name and the player's epithet.

Classes are read from what makes each one different from your other classes: its parameter curves and the skills it learns. On MZ's eight stock classes that gives eight different temperaments with no notetags at all, so a brand new project works on the first try. A notetag replaces the reading whenever you want to decide it yourself.

Ties get their own rule. If two outcomes match the answers equally, the quiz does not pick one quietly: a banner shows the tied outcomes, and one more question is asked between them only, one answer each.

How it was tested

In the real MZ engine on a pristine blank project: the plugin command placed on an event, played with the keyboard to the end, with the class change, variables, switches and a save and reload read back. Also with the three files loaded in reverse order, with the plugin enabled twice, with the drawing file switched off (the event is not left waiting), with keys and clicks spammed through a whole quiz, and with a save that still loads after the plugin is removed. The frame gate measured 2 draw calls a frame while a question waits.
