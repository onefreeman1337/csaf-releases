Rummage: I Spy scenes made from your own items and characters

A seek-and-find scene is a lovely thing to put in an RPG, and making one by hand means painting a background, painting every object, placing each one and checking nothing you ask for is buried out of sight. Rummage composes the scene from what your project already has.

I Spy scatters the items, weapons and armour in your database across one of eight surfaces the plugin draws (a dragon's hoard, a merchant's cloth, a tavern table, the forest floor, low tide, an alchemist's bench, a sunken wreck, a library desk), buries them in more than forty kinds of drawn clutter and writes their names on a parchment. Crowd fills a market square, a fair meadow or a quay with your characters, in front of stalls, tents or moored boats, and asks the player to find one person among recoloured lookalikes.

Every listed thing can be found, and Rummage checks it: it samples each one's outline, counts how much nothing above it covers, and moves the clutter until enough is in view. A click only counts on a part that can be seen.

How it was tested

In the real MZ engine with the three files loaded alphabetically (Rummage.js before the two it needs), with icons and characters made by our own studio. The proof was recomputed by brute force over 192 composed scenes and 1,536 listed things, and 1,198 clicks on hidden parts found nothing. Crowds of 30, 56 and 80 walkers were walked for 10,800 frames with nobody stepping behind the back row. The heaviest cases draw in 5 draw calls a frame (a 12-item Dragon Hoard on hard with the lens moving) and 7 (80 walkers).

Rummage sits with our other picture minigames: Jigsaw, Pendant and Rebus.
