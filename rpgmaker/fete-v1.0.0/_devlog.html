Fete: your own towns, dressed for their festivals

Every RPG has a festival day, and until now it meant copying the town map, decorating the copy by hand and keeping both maps in step. Fete dresses the map you already built.

It reads your houses (roofs, eaves, front walls), the gaps between them, your lanes and your square with the engine's own tile arithmetic, then strings bunting eave to eave and across the lanes, stands lamp posts along the fronts with lights between them, hangs banners and wreaths on free wall, rings the square with stalls round a centrepiece, and drifts leaves, petals, sky lanterns, motes or wisps over the town. Five festivals: Harvest Fair, Lantern Night, Spring Blossom, Midwinter and All Hallows.

Switch it on with a notetag, a switch or a plugin command, and it unspools on screen: strings run out post to post, posts and stalls rise. Tint the screen for night and the lights keep glowing. Nothing is written into your map, and every decoration is drawn in code.

How it was tested

In the real MZ engine with the plugins loaded in alphabetical order (Fete.js before the two it needs): on two of our own tilesets and on MZ's stock Outside tileset, every dressing, the unspool and fade transitions, regions and rectangles, switch bindings, solid posts that never cut a path (checked with a flood fill of the walkable map), saving, a 96 x 64 map with 64 houses, a looping map, 32 px tiles, a save loaded with the plugin removed. The heaviest scene, Lantern Night under a night tint, draws in 13 draw calls a frame with nothing uploaded per frame.

Fete pairs with Wrack: the same town burned and rebuilt, then celebrated.

Made with AI assistance (code and graphics).
