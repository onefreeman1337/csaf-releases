Cut the wood. It grows back. COPPICE is a free woodland incremental that plays in your browser. You take on a hazel wood nobody has cut in twenty years. Most of the stools are overstood, choking on their own poles and losing vigour. Bramble holds the gaps, and a few old oaks stand over it all. Hazel grows back from the stump when you cut it.

For centuries this wood was cut a patch at a time and never cleared, and you are going to bring it back. The one rule A stool's age when the crew cuts it decides what it gives: 2 to 4 years: beanpoles 5 to 9 years: hurdle rods 10 years and more: cordwood under 2 years: nothing but brash, and the stool is weakened Every cut stool grows again from the stump.

You mark stools, and the crew cuts them in autumn and winter, in the order you marked them. Three things that pull against each other The wood. Stools grow by their vigour and their light, and a grown oak shades the ones beside it. Past sixteen years a stool is overstood and declines, and an old weak one can collapse into bramble. Deer browse the fresh shoots unless you ring them with a dead hedge of brash.

You can layer a hazel rod into a gap to root a new stool, and plant oaks. The trade.
