HEADGATE is a game, paid, with a free browser demo on the store page.

You are a resurveyor, sent to Sufferance to re-establish section corners that were lost in 1873. Where a monument still stands in both years, your transit sights through time. And when you re-stage the past, the present redraws under your hands. A headgate is the structure that diverts water out of a river and into a ditch. In western water law it is the physical point at which a claim begins.

There is one at the end of this route, and whether the town has any right to it depends entirely on what you decide happened in 1851. What you actually do In the present, 1899, you walk. Continuous eight-way movement over seventeen places of dry country. Eight obstacles sit on the road between you and the headgate, and each one tells you plainly what it costs you now: dollars, or days, or worse.

In the past, 1873, you drag. At a monument that survives into your year the screen splits. The past sits above, a live strip of the present below. You take hold of people, objects and weather with the cursor and you move them, and the strip redraws at frame rate under your hand. Nothing is real until you press C. The redraw shows you the world changing.
