SOUNDER is a game, paid, with a free browser demo on the store page.

You are the night operator at a small railway telegraph office. The dispatcher writes an order on a slip. You key it out on the wire, by hand, one letter at a time, before two trains that cannot see each other arrive at the same piece of single track. There is no tutorial and nothing to memorise.

The code chart is on the screen the whole time — all thirty-six letters and numerals, drawn as the real thing — and the game never asks you to recall any of it. It asks you to read it while a clock runs. The key is one button A short press is a dot. A long press is a dash. A gap ends the letter.

That is the entire input scheme, and it is why this game exists on a Game Boy: two face buttons are a terrible keyboard and an excellent telegraph key. Hold it down and the cartridge sounds a tone for exactly as long as you hold it, which is how an operator hears the difference between a dot and a dash.

The office reads your message with the gaps ignored, and a message that matches the slip goes down the wire by itself, the moment the last letter lands. Stopping to find the next letter on the chart never costs you a letter: when the wire fills up with pauses, the earliest pause makes room.
