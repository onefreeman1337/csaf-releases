The balance tells you almost nothing Every packet carries an assay mark: a wax disc with an engraved device and a row of punched pips that declares its weight in scruples. You can count them. One packet’s true weight is a single scruple away from what its mark claims. The docket tells you whether it runs heavy, or light, or — on the harder cases — only that one of them is false.

The balance takes two marks a side and it says one thing: which way it tips. Never by how much. So a weighing is a question, and the answer is the gap between what the marks declare and what the beam actually does: These two declare five between them, and that one declares five. They should balance.
