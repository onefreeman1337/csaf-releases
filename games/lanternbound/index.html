The lamps along the moor road have gone out, one by one. Three of you walk out from Aldermoor to find out why, carrying one oil lantern between you. It burns down the whole time you are out there, and it is the only light in the world. LANTERNBOUND is a turn-based JRPG where the lantern is not a meter. It is the lighting, the fuel and the information, all at once.

While it is lit you can read what the thing in front of you is about to do. When it goes out, the telegraph is replaced by three question marks and you are guessing. The stack is the puzzle Every ability your party uses does its job and pushes a colour onto the lantern — EMBER, AZURE, VERDANT or PALE. The lantern holds three, in order, oldest falling off the bottom.

Enemies telegraph their attacks as a required sequence of those colours, and matching it breaks the attack before it lands. So the interesting decision is never "which attack hits hardest". It is what order the party acts in — because a well-aimed ability at the wrong moment buries the colour somebody else needed.

Four colours in a three-slot ordered stack is sixty-four reachable states from a roster of a dozen abilities.
