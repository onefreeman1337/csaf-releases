A panel arrives with a docket claiming a date. One region of it is painted in a colour that did not exist yet. Find it. CRAQUELURE is a Game Boy Color game — a 256 kB ROM you can put on a flash cart — about authenticating paintings. Play three lots free in your browser, right here on this page. The lamp is the whole game Every painting is divided into six regions, and each one is painted in a single pigment.

Under daylight you can only see the family: lead white, zinc white and titanium white are the same white. Natural ultramarine and French blue are the same blue. That is not the screen being limited. That is true of the actual paint. So you change the lamp. Under ultraviolet most of them come apart. Zinc white fluoresces a loud yellow-green. Titanium white goes dead black. Madder lake glows orange.

Prussian blue swallows the lamp completely. Under raking light you stop looking at colour and start looking at the surface. Old hand-ground mineral pigment is gritty. Bitumen tears itself into wide drying cracks. A modern machine-ground pigment is too even and barely crazes at all — and that absence is itself the tell. Then you check the dates against the docket.
