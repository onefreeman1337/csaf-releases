A witness mark is a real term from horology: the faint trace one part leaves on another, which a restorer reads to work out where things originally sat. It is physical evidence you reason backwards from, and it is what this game hands you. Five suspects stand in a lineup. A few marks describe the culprit — a hat, a coat, a collar, a face.

Cross off who cannot have done it. Accuse who is left. Three wrong arrests end the docket. New in 1.3.2 The pencil crosses no longer float over a blank screen. When you made an accusation, the screen was cleared before the crosses you had pencilled on the lineup were taken away, so for two frames they hung over an empty white screen.

Opening the legend from a lineup with crosses on it did the same, and so did opening the credits from the title. In all three places the crosses now leave first, so each of those changes of screen is one frame longer. The next screen is still drawn in front of you, as it always has been: the verdict and the credits in a frame or two, the legend one portrait at a time over about a second and a half.
