Intertitle is out for RPG Maker MZ.

It replaces the plain map name strip with a composed title card, and gives every boss a seal before the fight starts. Nothing is an image file: each card is drawn in code from data the project already has.

  • The map's tileset picks one of five constructions: an inked cartouche for the world map, a woodcut board outdoors, a gilt lacquer panel indoors, a carved stone lintel in a dungeon, a riveted plate aboard a ship or station.
  • The element its encounters strike with picks one of fifteen devices.
  • Its place in the map tree writes the epithet ("beneath Greyfell").
  • How dangerous its encounters are for the current party tints the rule and, at the top bands, changes its shape.
  • A return visit gets a plainer card than the first.
  • A boss (MZ's own Collapse Effect: Boss trait, or a notetag) gets a seal with its element, its weakness drawn as a fault line, and rays for how high it stands in the database.

On a blank project with no notetags, typing a Display Name on a map is enough for its card to appear, and the stock boss already gets a flame seal because it casts Fire. Every derivation can be overruled with a notetag.

Tested in the engine before release: the shipped zip installed into a blank MZ project, a twelve map demo world toured through MZ's own transfer path, six boss battles held and released, and the frame cost measured over 300 stepped frames per scenario (0 texture uploads, under half a millisecond of plugin time).

Three readable .js files, two OFL typefaces, one commercial licence. Made with AI assistance: code and graphics yes, sounds no, and the short default boss epithets are generated text.
