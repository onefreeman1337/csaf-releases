104 dialogue sounds. 13 speakers, not one blip pitched 13 ways.

Text advance blips are the sound almost every RPG and visual novel needs, and almost nobody sells properly. The usual pack gives you one blip, and a cast made by pitching that one blip up and down, so a party of six reads as one instrument playing a scale.

This pack is 13 genuinely different voices. Each one is a different synthesis method, not a different frequency: a ringing two pole resonator, a formant pair excited by noise, a bit crusher, a narrow pulse behind a low filter, a wide detune with a long tail.

Hear it first

There is a free demo on this page and it is not a teaser. It contains four complete voices, chosen by measurement from the darkest to the brightest end of the cast, with every one of the 8 cues included and copied unaltered. Play their four blips back to back. If those do not read as four different speakers to you, the rest will not either, and you will know before you pay.

The cast

  • Narrator. Deliberately unremarkable. The voice that is not a character. 348 Hz
  • Merchant. Round and companionable, two detuned tones beating gently. 435 Hz
  • Beast. Low, noisy and inarticulate. Not a talker. 461 Hz
  • Rogue. Clipped short and dry. Says less per glyph than anyone. 581 Hz
  • Knight. Blunt and square edged, with weight behind it. 585 Hz
  • Noble. Clean, ringing and unhurried. The most tonal voice here. 686 Hz
  • Scholar. Measured and slightly muffled, as if speaking over a book. 837 Hz
  • Spirit. Thin, detuned and trailing. The longest decay in the cast. 865 Hz
  • Youth. Light and quick, with a little body under it. 1028 Hz
  • Child. Small, bright and very short. The highest voice in the cast. 1294 Hz
  • Herald. Brass forward, built from a formant pair rather than a filter. 1630 Hz
  • Crone. Breathy and grained, a formant excited by noise rather than a tone. 2904 Hz
  • Automaton. Quantised and rigid. Steps rather than glides. 3908 Hz

Those figures are the mean brightness of each voice's own per glyph tick, measured off the shipped WAV files. The build compares all 78 pairs and refuses to write the pack unless every pair is separated, by at least 12 percent in brightness or 25 percent in length, and unless the cast spans at least 2 times overall. It measured 10.67 times, with 0 pairs failing. That check runs over the audio you get, not over a claim in a manifest.

Every speaker ships the same 8 cues

  • blip_01. tick for one glyph, take 1
  • blip_02. tick for one glyph, take 2
  • blip_03. tick for one glyph, take 3
  • soft. the same voice lowered, for asides and whispers
  • loud. the same voice raised, for shouting and emphasis
  • question. rising inflection, for a line ending in ?
  • exclaim. emphatic hit, for a line ending in !
  • advance. page turn / next line, in this voice

Three takes of the per glyph tick, because a blip fires once per character and one file machine guns inside a single sentence. A lower reading of the same voice for an aside and a higher, longer one for a raised voice. A rising cue for a line ending in a question mark and a hard one for an exclamation, because the blip systems players remember change the tick on punctuation. And a page turn in that voice's own timbre.

The uniformity is an assertion in the generator rather than a convention: the build refuses to run if any voice is missing any cue, so none of them is the one that got rushed.

Fit

  • WAV and OGG for every file, 16 bit, 44.1 kHz, mono.
  • Filenames are voice then cue, so a dialogue system that already knows who is talking needs one string concatenation.
  • One loudness band. The inner 80 percent sits inside 0.7 dB. The voices are meant to sound unlike each other; they are not meant to be at different volumes, so switching speaker never changes how loud the textbox is. The soft and loud cues differ in pitch and length, not level, so set their volume in your game for a real whisper or shout.
  • 13 declared variation sets, one per voice, so you can load a set and pick at random without auditioning anything.
  • Nothing loops, and no file carries loop points. A looping dialogue blip is a defect.
  • pack_proof.json ships with the pack: 945 checks, 0 failed, with the per file measurements behind every number on this page.

Honest limits

These are synthesised character voices in the blip tradition, not recorded speech and not a vocal synthesiser. They say that someone is talking, the way a text advance blip does. They do not say words. And the measurements above are SIGNAL measurements: no clipping, no silence, trimmed edges, one loudness band and the cast separation. They are not a claim that it sounds good. That is what the free demo is for.
