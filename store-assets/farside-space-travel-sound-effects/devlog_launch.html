Farside: Space Travel Sound Effects is out. 121 sounds, shipped as WAV and OGG with loop points on every loopable file, a naming manifest and per engine import notes.

The pack is a grid, 4 drive values by 5 phase values (jump drive, warp bubble, jump gate and fold drive; spool up, jump, drop out, misjump and cooldown), so a game asks for the cell it needs and gets it. All 20 cells ship real audio (20 files), and along each axis every pair of cells was compared from the shipped files: 40 along the rows and 30 down the columns, each at least 9% apart in brightness or 18% in length. Indistinct pairs: 0. Change the drive and the sound changes; change the phase and it changes too.

The pack ships 8 escalation ladders: ordered ramps, not bags of cues. 26 rungs were resolved (30 files with their takes) and 18 adjacent steps checked: every step moves in the declared direction by at least 8% in brightness or 12% in length, and every ladder spans at least 1.5x end to end. A ladder that stalls or reverses fails the build.

The pack ships 7 mechanisms as whole lifecycles rather than as single cues. 24 state files were resolved and 31 within mechanism pairs were compared. 1 state is loopable and it carries real loop points, read out of the rendered file rather than out of the manifest. No state pair sits closer than 9% in brightness or 18% in length, which is what stops a lifecycle being one gesture under several names.

Royalty free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything here is synthesised from scratch in code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence sitting behind it. The plain language licence ships in the download. The only thing you may not do is resell the pack itself as a pack.

There is a free demo on the page: a real subset of the pack, not a teaser reel. Download it and listen before you decide.

What was measured, and it is in pack_proof.json inside the download so you can check it yourself: 1110 checks, 0 failed. The inner 80 percent of the pack sits inside 1.19 dB of loudness, so swapping one cue for another does not jump. 13 variation families are declared and each was checked for real variation rather than the same render twice.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. Code and graphics are AI assisted; the audio is not: every sample is procedural synthesis written in Node, with no generative audio model anywhere in the pipeline.
