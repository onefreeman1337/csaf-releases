One chain, every dish

A cooking game repeats one loop all day: prepare it, put it on, wait, take it off, serve it. Every dish here ships that loop under the same names:

  • prep: two takes, the dish made ready its own way (chopped, cracked, whisked, kneaded, rubbed, rinsed or scooped)
  • add: into the pot, the pan or the oven
  • cook: a seamless loop for as long as your timer runs
  • done: a soft rising cue in the dish's own sparkle, never a jingle
  • plate: served onto crockery
  • serve: slid across and set down on the table

So you wire a dish once and only its name changes: soup_done, egg_done, tea_done. Checked from the shipped files, not promised: all 8 dishes carry exactly this chain, and the 120 pairs of stages inside each dish all differ.

Warn before it burns

A pan ships four doneness loops, raw, cooking, ready and burning, each brighter and rougher than the last, and a kettle four stages from cold to whistling. Wire them to your cooking timer and crossfade up the ladder as a dish cooks: the burning rung is the warning before it is ruined. Measured from the shipped files: 6 steps, each at least 8% brighter than the one below, failing steps: 0.

The dishes

  • Vegetable soup: vegetables chopped on the board, tipped into a pot of stock that simmers.
  • Hearty stew: heavier chopping, a thick pot that bubbles slow and low.
  • Fried egg: a shell cracked on the rim, the egg hitting a hot pan and sizzling.
  • Pancakes: batter whisked in a bowl, poured into a pan, a soft sizzle with bubbles.
  • Bread: dough kneaded and slapped on the board, a tray slid into the oven.
  • Roast: salt and herbs rubbed in, a heavy tin into the oven, fat spitting as it roasts.
  • Rice: grains rinsed in a bowl, poured into the pot, steam lifting the lid.
  • Tea: dry leaves scooped into the pot, water poured over, a quiet steep.

Fit

  • WAV and OGG for every file, 16 bit, 44.1 kHz, mono, with a naming manifest listing every file by dish and stage.
  • 18 seamless loops of 4 seconds with loop points in both formats, and none on a one shot.
  • 24 variation sets whose takes differ in length and in brightness, so the tenth chop of the morning is not the first one again.
  • One loudness band: the inner 80 percent of the pack sits inside 0.91 dB, so a roasting tin and a menu tick share a mix.
  • Import notes for Unity, Unreal, Godot, GameMaker, RPG Maker and the web, and pack_proof.json with a sha256 and a measurement for every file: 1163 checks, 0 failed.

Honest limits

These are synthesised, stylised sounds designed for games, not field recordings. The figures above are signal measurements, not a claim that anything sounds good; that is what the free demo is for. No voices, no animals, no crops or field tools (those live in Hearthlight, the farm pack) and nothing musical: a dish being done is a soft sparkle, never a jingle.

AI disclosure, stated truthfully: the code that generates these sounds was written with AI assistance. The sounds themselves are self contained procedural synthesis by Core Systems Asset Factory, with no generative audio model at any point. The cover art and store graphics are made by AI written code from the pack's own waveforms.
