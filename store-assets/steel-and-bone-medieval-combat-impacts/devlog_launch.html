Steel and Bone — Medieval Combat Impacts is out. 144 sounds, shipped as WAV and OGG, a naming manifest and per-engine import notes.

The pack is a grid of material pairs, 8 strikers by 6 receivers: every impact is filed by what hits what, so a combat system asks for "axe on mail" and gets it. All 48 pairs ship real audio (120 files), and along each axis every pair of cells was compared from the shipped files: 120 along the rows and 168 down the columns, each at least 9% apart in brightness or 18% in length. Indistinct pairs: 0. The armour changes the sound, and so does the weapon.

Royalty-free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything here is synthesised from scratch in code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence sitting behind it. The plain-language licence ships in the download. The only thing you may not do is resell the pack itself as a pack.

There is a free demo on the page — a real subset of the pack, not a teaser reel. Download it and listen before you decide.

What was measured, and it is in pack_proof.json inside the download so you can check it yourself: 1305 checks, 0 failed. Loudness sits inside 2.43 dB across the pack, so nothing jumps when you swap one cue for another. 58 variation families are declared and each was checked for real variation rather than the same render twice.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. Code and graphics are AI-assisted; the audio is not — every sample is procedural synthesis written in Node, with no generative-audio model anywhere in the pipeline.
