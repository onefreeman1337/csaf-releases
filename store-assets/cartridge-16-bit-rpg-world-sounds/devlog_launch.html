Cartridge: 133 16-bit RPG World Sounds and BGS Loops is out. 133 sounds in WAV and OGG for a retro RPG: the half of a game's sound that a map plays. 33 seamless background loops, the chests, doors and switches a map is full of, the terrain under the party's feet, and the creatures that roam it.

The loops are BGS ready. Rain light, steady and heavy, a thunderstorm, wind from the plains to a blizzard, forests, rivers, a waterfall, a seashore, caves, a lava cavern, a dungeon, a sewer, a town square, a tavern, a castle hall, a campfire and more. Every loop carries the LOOPSTART and LOOPLENGTH tags RPG Maker MV and MZ read, so Play BGS loops it with no plugin, and smpl points in the WAV for Godot.

One modelled 16 bit console chain makes every file. A small instrument bank stored in a 4 bit block code (32,256 bytes for all 22 instruments), played at a pitch through a Gaussian interpolation, a noise generator for rain, wind and breath, envelopes, never more than eight voices at once, an echo unit for caves and halls, out at 32,000 Hz. It is built from the general shape of that era's hardware, not copied from any game.

Every mechanism is whole. Three chests and two doors that open, close and rattle when locked, a dungeon gate, a lever, a floor switch, a pressure plate and a push block that loops while it moves. 10 mechanisms, 24 state pairs compared, none the same sound renamed. Monster yells and footfalls come in four sizes, darker as the creature grows.

Royalty free. Commercial use. No attribution required. There is a free demo on the page: 12 sounds, with rain, forest, cave and town loops, a chest, a door and a monster. The middle 80 percent of files sit within 0.56 dB of each other in loudness.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. The code and the cover were made with AI tools; the sounds were not: every one is procedural synthesis written in code, with no generative audio model anywhere in the pipeline. There is no music and there are no voices. These measurements are signal checks, so please listen to the demo to judge the sound for yourself.
