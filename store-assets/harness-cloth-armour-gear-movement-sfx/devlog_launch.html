Harness — The Body Layer: Cloth, Armour and Gear Movement is out. 152 sounds, shipped as WAV and OGG with loop points on every loopable file, a naming manifest and per-engine import notes.

The pack is a grid, 6 garment values by 4 gait values (cloth, heavy cloak, leather, mail, plate and adventurer's kit; walk, run, sneak and land), so a game asks for the cell it needs and gets it. All 24 cells ship real audio (72 files), and along each axis every pair of cells was compared from the shipped files: 36 along the rows and 60 down the columns, each at least 9% apart in brightness or 18% in length. Indistinct pairs: 0. Change the garment and the sound changes; change the gait and it changes too.

It also ships 2 more grids: garment by move (6 x 6) and garment by action (6 x 2). Inside each, every pair of cells that differ in one thing was compared from the shipped files: 96 along the rows and 120 down the columns, 48 files in all, each pair at least 9% apart in brightness or 18% in length. Failing grids: 0.

Royalty-free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything here is synthesised from scratch in code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence sitting behind it. The plain-language licence ships in the download. The only thing you may not do is resell the pack itself as a pack.

There is a free demo on the page — a real subset of the pack, not a teaser reel. Download it and listen before you decide.

What was measured, and it is in pack_proof.json inside the download so you can check it yourself: 1391 checks, 0 failed. Loudness sits inside 1.02 dB across the pack, so nothing jumps when you swap one cue for another. 31 variation families are declared and each was checked for real variation rather than the same render twice.
