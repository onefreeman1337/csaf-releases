Tilt: 184 Pinball and Pachinko Sound Effects is out. 184 sounds in WAV and OGG for ball and table games, for any engine: both flippers firing, held and dropping, pop bumpers in three voices, slingshots, drop targets and their bank reset, the spinner, the plunger, the drain, tilt, the replay knocker and score chimes.

One table, heard close. A steel ball on a plywood playfield inside a wooden cabinet: coils firing into steel stops, rubber rings, plastic caps and ramps, steel wire rails. Beside it is a pachinko machine, with tiny steel balls over brass nails, tulips, the big gate and the payout into the tray.

The ball rolling is a grid. Wood, plastic ramp and steel wire, each at slow, medium and fast, as 9 seamless loops. Each surface is built on its own, so it is not one loop filtered three ways, and the loops of one surface share one length so you can crossfade by speed. Every pair along a row or a column is compared by the gate: 18 pairs, 0 indistinct.

Peg boards climb. Peg hits in glass, brass and stone are each a ten step ladder, one file per hit in a shot, each step brighter than the last by a fixed ratio that is no musical scale. Your game plays them one at a time, so wire the step to the hit count. 14 ladders in all, including flipper strength, launch strength, the spinner and the bonus count, all measured from the shipped files.

Royalty free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything is synthesised from code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence behind it. There is a free demo on the page: 20 sounds, a whole ball from the plunger to the drain, every state of one flipper, a drop target bank, three peg steps and two pachinko sounds. 24 loops ship with loop points in the WAV and the OGG tags, and the middle 80 percent of files sit within 1.49 dB of each other in loudness.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. The code and the cover were made with AI tools; the sounds were not: every one is procedural synthesis written in code, with no generative audio model anywhere in the pipeline. There are no voices and no tunes. These measurements are signal checks, so please listen to the demo to judge the sound for yourself.
