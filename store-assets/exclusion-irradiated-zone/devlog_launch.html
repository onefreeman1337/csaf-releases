Exclusion — The Irradiated Zone is out. 103 sounds, shipped as WAV and OGG with loop points on every loopable file, a naming manifest and per-engine import notes.

The pack is a grid, 3 striker values by 4 material values (boot kick, steel pipe and thrown stone; corrugated sheet, oil drum, rebar and chain-link fence), so a game asks for the cell it needs and gets it. All 12 cells ship real audio (12 files), and along each axis every pair of cells was compared from the shipped files: 18 along the rows and 12 down the columns, each at least 9% apart in brightness or 18% in length. Indistinct pairs: 0. Change the striker and the sound changes; change the material and it changes too.

Royalty-free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything here is synthesised from scratch in code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence sitting behind it. The plain-language licence ships in the download. The only thing you may not do is resell the pack itself as a pack.

There is a free demo on the page — a real subset of the pack, not a teaser reel. Download it and listen before you decide.

What was measured, and it is in pack_proof.json inside the download so you can check it yourself: 967 checks, 0 failed. Loudness sits inside 4.79 dB across the pack, so nothing jumps when you swap one cue for another. 13 variation families are declared and each was checked for real variation rather than the same render twice.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. Code and graphics are AI-assisted; the audio is not — every sample is procedural synthesis written in Node, with no generative-audio model anywhere in the pipeline.
