Feudal Japan, from one palette

106 sound effects for a game set in feudal Japan: the menus, the katana, the shinobi's kit, the castle, the shrine and the war camp, all struck, slid and rung from the same few materials.

Licence, up front

Royalty free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Use it in as many games as you like, on any platform. The one thing you may not do is resell or redistribute the sounds themselves as a sound pack. The plain language terms ship as LICENSE.txt, with a copy and paste credit line for anyone who wants one. Everything is synthesised from code, so there is no upstream sample licence behind it.

One palette, one world

Tamahagane steel for the blades and guards, small iron for shuriken, kunai and armour plates, hinoki cypress and bamboo for floors, gates and knockers, paper and silk for shoji, scrolls and banners, temple bronze for the bell and the gong, lacquer for boxes and menus, and drum skin for the war drum. Every cue is made from these, so a Sengoku game sounds like one place rather than a folder of borrowed clips.

Three ladders a game can index

  • The katana draw, by pace: quick, steady and slow, each draw longer than the last.
  • The nightingale floor, by weight: a light, a medium and a heavy step, each darker than the last, so a stealth game can map footfall to weight.
  • The war drum, by force: soft, firm and full, each hit brighter than the last.

Measured from the shipped files: 6 steps, each at least 8% in brightness or 12% in length in the declared direction, failing steps: 0.

Two shinobi tools as whole lifecycles

  • The smoke bomb: thrown, bursting, drifting on a seamless loop while the ninja hides, clearing.
  • The grappling hook: thrown, catching, climbing, letting go.

Checked, not promised: the 12 pairs of states inside each tool all differ.

Four blades meet steel (katana, wakizashi, naginata and yari, two takes each), shuriken and kunai strike wood, paper and armour, the gate groans open and slams, the shishi odoshi knocks, shrine bells shake, an ofuda talisman ignites, banners flap and lamellar armour rattles. Five ambience loops: a castle at night, a bamboo grove, a shrine in the rain, the war camp and cicadas at dusk.

Fit

  • WAV and OGG for every file, 16 bit, 44.1 kHz, mono, with a naming manifest listing every file.
  • 6 seamless loops of 4 seconds with loop points in both formats, and none on a one shot.
  • 25 variation sets whose takes differ in length and in brightness, so the tenth shuriken is not the first one again.
  • One loudness band: the inner 80 percent of the pack sits inside 1.2 dB, so a temple bell and a menu tick share a mix.
  • Import notes for Unity, Unreal, Godot, GameMaker, RPG Maker and the web, and pack_proof.json with a sha256 and a measurement for every file: 970 checks, 0 failed.

Honest limits

These are synthesised, stylised sounds designed for games, not field recordings. The figures above are signal measurements, not a claim that anything sounds good; that is what the free demo is for. No voices, no animals and nothing musical: no shamisen, koto or shakuhachi, and the bell, the gong and the drum are single struck objects, never a tune.

AI disclosure, stated truthfully: the code that generates these sounds was written with AI assistance. The sounds themselves are self contained procedural synthesis by Core Systems Asset Factory, with no generative audio model at any point. The cover art and store graphics are made by AI written code from the pack's own waveforms.
