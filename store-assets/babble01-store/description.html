650 dialogue sounds. 10 speakers, each talking in a made up language of its own, in 5 moods.

Text advance audio in the babble style. As a line of dialogue types out, the speaker talks in synthesised syllables, a consonant and a vowel each, so the line sounds like speech in an invented language instead of a beep. Every speaker has its own consonants, its own vowels and its own twelve syllables, and a letter map so the same word always sounds the same in that voice.

Hear it first

The cast

  • Bram, a giant. The deepest and slowest voice in the cast. Speaks with b, m, d and g. 64 Hz, 255 ms per syllable.
  • Grask, a warrior. Low and blunt, with a hard, pressed edge to every syllable. Speaks with g, k, d and r. 86 Hz, 122 ms per syllable.
  • Mott, an elder. Low, slow and breathy, with a tremble in the voice. Speaks with h, m, w and f. 103 Hz, 227 ms per syllable.
  • Unit, a robot. A buzzing tone that steps between notes instead of gliding. Speaks with t, k, d and b. 133 Hz, 85 ms per syllable.
  • Bellamy, a merchant. A bright, forward voice in the middle of the cast, at an easy talking pace. Speaks with b, d, g, s and r. 147 Hz, 121 ms per syllable.
  • Wren, the hero. A warm, even voice in the middle of the cast. Speaks with l, m, n, t, k and s. 179 Hz, 123 ms per syllable.
  • Seraphine, a noble. Unhurried and refined, never rushing a word. Speaks with s, l, v and n. 219 Hz, 166 ms per syllable.
  • Wisp, a spirit. Half breath, half voice, drifting slowly with a waver. Speaks with h, sh, f and w. 250 Hz, 229 ms per syllable.
  • Pip, a child. High, and quick to get the words out. Speaks with p, b, t, n and y. 296 Hz, 85 ms per syllable.
  • Snik, a trickster. Tiny, sly and fast, the highest voice in the cast. Speaks with k, n, sh and s. 358 Hz, 85 ms per syllable.

Pitch and syllable length are measured off the shipped files.

What every speaker ships

  • 12 syllables in each of 5 moods: neutral, happy, angry, sad, question. The whole set in every mood, so the letter map still works when a line is shouted or asked.
  • 5 cues: open, advance, close, hmm, laugh.
  • A letter map in letter_map.json: every letter a to z goes to the syllable in that speaker's language that sounds most like it.
  • A stride table: play a syllable every N letters at 30, 45 or 60 characters per second.
  • A Godot 4 helper node (babble_voice.gd): set a speaker and mood, call type_glyph() per character. Tested in a blank Godot 4.7 project.

Measured, not promised

  • Syllables stay separate at text speed. Three lines typed in every speaker and mood at 30, 45 and 60 characters per second: 3175 of 3175 syllable boundaries dip clearly between syllables. A syllable on every single letter at 60 per second smears into a drone, and the stride table exists to stop that.
  • The moods do what they say for every speaker: happy higher and quicker, angry brighter and quicker, sad lower, slower and darker, a question rising at the end. 40 of 40 checks hold.
  • Real vowels. Each speaker's vowels come from a modelled vocal tract and measure in the right order: 55 of 55 comparisons hold.
  • A cast, not one voice at ten pitches. All 45 pairs of speakers are separated in brightness or syllable length, in every mood.
  • One loudness band. The inner 80 percent sits inside 0.21 dB, so switching speaker never changes how loud the text box is.
  • WAV and OGG for every file, 16 bit, 44.1 kHz, mono. Nothing loops.

Honest limits

These are invented languages, not words. Nothing here says anything in English or any real language. The measurements above are signal measurements, not a claim that a voice sounds charming. That is what the free demo is for.

AI disclosure, stated truthfully: the code that generates these sounds was written with AI assistance. The sounds themselves are self contained procedural synthesis, a glottal source and formant filters evaluated in code, with no generative audio or voice model at any point. The cover and store graphics are AI generated.
