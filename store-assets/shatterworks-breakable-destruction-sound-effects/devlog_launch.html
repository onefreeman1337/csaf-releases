Shatterworks: Breakables by Material and Break Stage is out. 110 sounds, shipped as WAV and OGG, a naming manifest and per-engine import notes.

The pack ships 10 materials and every one of them carries exactly the same 6-stage chain (stress, crack, break_light, break_medium, break_heavy and settle), in the same order, under the same naming — resolved to 110 real files. That is the whole product: you read the stage list once and then wire it 10 times. It is checked mechanically rather than promised, and the build refuses the pack if any material's chain differs from the canonical one by a single stage or a single position.

The pack ships 10 escalation ladders: ordered ramps, not bags of cues. 60 rungs were resolved and 20 adjacent steps checked: every step moves in the declared direction by at least 12% in length, and every ladder spans at least 1.5x end to end. A ladder that stalls or reverses fails the build.

The pack ships 10 materials as whole lifecycles rather than as single cues. 110 state files were resolved and 150 within-material pairs were compared. No state pair sits closer than 9% in brightness or 18% in length, which is what stops a lifecycle being one gesture under several names.

Royalty-free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything here is synthesised from scratch in code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence sitting behind it. The plain-language licence ships in the download. The only thing you may not do is resell the pack itself as a pack.

There is a free demo on the page — a real subset of the pack, not a teaser reel. Download it and listen before you decide.

What was measured, and it is in pack_proof.json inside the download so you can check it yourself: 1001 checks, 0 failed. The inner 80 percent of the pack sits inside 1.97 dB of loudness, so swapping one cue for another does not jump. 50 variation families are declared and each was checked for real variation rather than the same render twice.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. Code and graphics are AI-assisted; the audio is not — every sample is procedural synthesis written in Node, with no generative-audio model anywhere in the pipeline.
