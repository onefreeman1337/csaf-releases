Balloon: 120 Emote Sound Effects is out. 120 sounds in WAV and OGG for the small balloon a character pops over its head: exclamation, question, music note, heart, anger, sweat, frustration, silence, light bulb and Zzz, plus surprise, sparkle, gloom and blush. It works in any engine, and it ships a small RPG Maker MZ plugin.

An emote is a gesture, a voice is an instrument. A question rises and hangs, gloom sags, silence is three small dots. Each of the 14 gestures is played by 4 voices (soft cozy, retro chip, anime and wood and paper), with two takes of each, so a game picks one voice and every balloon in it speaks the same language. 448 pairs of cells that differ in one thing were compared from the shipped files, each at least 9% apart in brightness or 18% in length. Failing pairs: 0.

Why a plugin ships in a sound pack. The MZ engine draws a balloon and plays nothing, so every Show Balloon Icon normally needs its own Play SE command beside it. Turn the plugin on and pick a voice, and every balloon plays its cue, including balloons from other plugins that use the engine's own request. A note tag gives one character its own voice, and two plugin commands change the voice or turn the cues off for a scene. It was run in the MZ 1.10.0 runtime in a fresh project: 112 id and voice cells through the engine's own command, in both of its ogg decoders, 0 problems.

Built to the length of the balloon. A stock balloon is on screen for 76 frames, 1.27 seconds at 60 frames a second. The longest file here is 1.16 seconds, so no sound outlasts its picture. The inner 80 percent of the pack sits inside 0.87 dB of loudness, so swapping the voice needs no gain riding.

Royalty free. Commercial use. No attribution required. Everything is synthesised from code, so there are no samples and no upstream licence behind it. There is a free demo on the page: 18 sounds and the plugin set up to play them. Listen before you buy; that is what it is for.

Made by Core Systems Asset Factory. The code and the cover were made with AI tools; the sounds were not: every one is procedural synthesis written in code, with no generative audio model anywhere in the pipeline. There are no voice syllables and nothing musical. Nobody claims these sound good; the figures above are signal measurements.
