Foxhole is now on this page: 14 low poly Second World War soldiers of four armies in the PlayStation 1 look, on the same skeleton, clip names and shader as the Fort Vale soldiers, the Crosstown civilians, the Ashfall raiders and the Syndicate gangsters. That makes 70 characters on this page, 210 with the palette skins.

The cast: US rifleman, German rifleman, British rifleman, Soviet rifleman, US sergeant, German machine gunner, Soviet sniper, British officer, US paratrooper, German squad leader, Soviet tank crewman, British commando, US medic and Soviet field medic. American, British, German and Soviet infantry of the 1940s. Each is a different body and a different painted uniform, about 1,500 triangles, one 256 px texture per skin, 45 clips. Every character is a grown adult.

Helmets you can see from across a map: every helmet and cap is modelled geometry, not paint: a round dome, a flat dish, a flared steel helmet, a high Soviet dome, a padded tank helmet, a peaked service cap, a field cap and a beret. The uniforms are plain: no insignia, badges, patches, flags or emblems, and the medics carry plain bags with no red cross.

Honest limit: the hands are simple mittens and nothing is held, and no rifle is painted on, so the six pistol clips aim an empty hand until you attach your own weapon.

Three palettes each: the uniform changes colour; faces, hair, boots and helmets keep their paint.

How we tested it: a blank Godot 4.7 project imported all 42 Foxhole GLBs, and the shader compiled and drew on every character under Forward+, Compatibility and Mobile. Every FBX and OBJ was reopened in Blender. Every character was looked at from four angles and in four clips, every skin in engine, and a second, independent AI review looked at the pack four times; every fix it marked necessary was made before release.
