Night Beat is a pack of 14 low poly PS1-style police officers, detectives and SWAT for Godot 4, Unity and Unreal, rigged and animated, with a free sampler on this page. Every character has 45 animation clips on one humanoid skeleton, 3 uniform palettes (42 to place), 1492-1498 triangles and a 256 px texture per skin, in GLB, FBX, OBJ and Blender formats. Use them in any number of commercial games, no attribution required.

A whole precinct for a PlayStation era crime, cop or horror game: the beat, the detectives, the tactical team, the county and the station.

What you get

  • 14 characters, each a different body and a different painted uniform: patrol officer, homicide detective, SWAT pointman, sergeant, SWAT breacher, motorcycle officer, state trooper, plainclothes detective, riot officer, county deputy, forensic technician, federal agent, dispatcher, prison guard.
  • Real heights, 1.70 m to 1.94 m. The scale sits on the armature, so the clips scale with it.
  • 3 palette skins each, 42 characters to place. One garment or uniform changes colour: the patrol officer's navy goes county tan or night black, the sergeant's light blue shirt goes tan or white with her trousers following, the motorcycle officer's high visibility vest goes orange or blue. Skin, faces, belts, helmets and boots keep their paint.
  • 45 animation clips on every character plus a T-pose, with the ones a police game needs: the six pistol clips (idle, aim up, aim down, aim, shoot, reload), a flashlight idle, talking, driving, crouch, sprint, roll, punches, chest and head hits and a death, plus sitting.
  • 1492-1498 triangles per character and one 256 x 256 px texture per skin, quantised to 15-bit colour for nearest filtering.
  • Per character: GLB with every clip, GLB per extra skin, FBX with clips, OBJ + MTL, PNG textures and the Blender source.
  • A PSX shader for Godot 4 (godot_psx.gdshader): vertex snapping, affine texture warp, 15-bit colour with dithering, distance fog.

Honest limits: no weapon or flashlight models ship and the hands are simple mittens, so the pistol clips aim an empty hand: attach your own pistol to the hand bone. Badges are plain, with no text or real agency marks. Helmets and armour plates are painted on the body, not modelled.

In your project

Godot 4: copy models/ and shaders/ into the project, instance a _skin0.glb, put the PSX shader on the mesh. Tested here in a blank Godot 4.7.2 project: every GLB imports, the shader compiles on every character, the clips play.

Unity 6 / Unreal 5: import the FBX and set the textures to nearest filtering. The skeleton is a standard humanoid, built to retarget. These two engines were not run here; the FBX files were re-imported in Blender to check that mesh, 53 bones and every clip arrive.

Documentation: the full Readme has the import notes, every height and the clip list.
