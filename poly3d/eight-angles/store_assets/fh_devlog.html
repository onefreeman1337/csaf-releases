Eight Angles is now on this page: 14 retro FPS enemies, each shipped two ways: as billboard sprite sheets in eight directions and as a low poly PS1 style model on the same skeleton, clip names and shader as the Hollow Congregation cultists and the Saint Oswin asylum cast. That makes 42 characters on this page, 126 with the palette skins.

The bestiary: possessed trooper, frost witch, mutant brute, cyborg gunner, cult acolyte, ghoul, shock trooper, fire fiend, berserker, rune knight, hazmat spitter, bone shaman, bronze automaton and husk marine. Every one is a grown adult.

The sprites: 210 sheets and 11,424 frames of idle, walk, attack, pain and death in eight rotations, for each of the three skins, at one pixel scale with hard alpha, and a sprites.json per enemy with frame size and pivot. A small Godot 4 node picks the right row from the camera angle, and the demo scene puts all 14 in one room: open the Eight Angles folder in Godot and press F5.

The models: about 1,500 triangles each, one 256 px texture per skin, 45 clips, GLB, FBX, OBJ and the Blender source.

How we tested it: the Godot 4.7 demo ran from a copy of the shipped pack with every enemy turning to the right row, a blank Godot project imported every model, every FBX and OBJ was reopened in Blender, every sprite sheet was decoded and counted, and a second, independent AI review looked at the pack four times; every fix it marked necessary was made before release. Count so far: 19 PSX packs on 11 pages, 7 of those pages sold.

Made with AI tools: every texture is an AI image model's painting projected onto the mesh, and the bodies and sprites were built and rendered by scripts an AI system wrote. The skeleton and clips are the CC0 Quaternius Universal Animation Library.
