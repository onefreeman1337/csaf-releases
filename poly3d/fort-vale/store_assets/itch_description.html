Fort Vale is a pack of 14 low poly PS1-style modern soldier characters for Godot 4, Unity and Unreal. Every character is rigged to one humanoid skeleton with 45 animation clips, comes in 3 camouflage colourways (42 characters to place), and has 1493-1499 triangles and one 256 px texture per skin, in GLB, FBX, OBJ and Blender formats. Use them in any number of commercial games, no attribution required. A free sampler (the rifleman, every skin and clip) is on this page.

The garrison of a fictional modern army base, for PlayStation era tactical shooters: the squad, the specialists and the people who run the base. Plain unmarked uniforms, no real army's flags or insignia.

What you get

  • 14 soldiers, each a different body AND a different painted uniform: rifleman, field officer, combat medic, special operator, helicopter pilot, combat engineer, radio operator, recruit, military police, veteran sergeant, quartermaster, grenadier, sniper, scout.
  • 3 camouflage colourways each = 42 characters to place. The uniform and its gear change colour together (woodland to desert to urban grey, a sage flight suit to navy or tan, khaki service dress to olive or slate); faces, hair, boots, armbands and face paint keep their painted colours.
  • 45 animation clips on every character plus a T-pose: idle, walk, jog, sprint, crouch walk and crouch idle, jump, roll, 6 pistol clips (idle, aim up / level / down, shoot, reload), two hit reactions, a death, punches, driving, sit, swim, push, pick up, interact, kneeling repair and more.
  • Honest limit: the CC0 clip library has no rifle animations, so the rifles are painted slung across the backs and the soldiers fight with the pistol set. Attach your own weapon meshes to the hand bones.
  • 1493-1499 triangles per character, one 256 x 256 px texture per skin, quantised to 15-bit colour for nearest filtering. The PlayStation look, not a modern model with a filter on top.
  • Per character: GLB with every clip, GLB per extra skin, FBX with clips, OBJ + MTL, PNG textures, and the Blender source file.
  • A PSX shader for Godot 4 (godot_psx.gdshader): vertex snapping, affine texture warp, 15-bit colour with dithering, distance fog. Every setting is a slider.
  • Readme with import notes, LICENSE, NOTICE.

In your project

Godot 4: copy models/ and shaders/ into the project, instance a _skin0.glb, put the PSX shader on the mesh. Tested here in a blank Godot 4.7.2 project: every GLB imports, the shader compiles on every character, the clips play (Godot names Walk_Loop as Walk and loops it). The in-engine images on this page are that test.

Unity 6 / Unreal 5: import the FBX (skeleton and clips included) and set the textures to point / nearest filtering. The skeleton is a standard humanoid, built to retarget (Unity Humanoid, Unreal IK Rig). These two engines were not run here; the FBX files were re-imported in Blender to check that mesh, 53 bones and every clip arrive.
