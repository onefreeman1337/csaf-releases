A ruined classical sanctuary: a colonnade of coursed limestone ashlar, a courtyard of weathered flags, a mosaic plaza with a cistern sunk into the middle of it, the spill of a collapsed terracotta roof, root-broken earth where the paving has gone — and dry summer grass taking all of it back.

564 of those tiles — 81% of the pack — are autotile transitions, because the transitions are the part that takes the time and the part most packs leave out. Why the grass is not green A ruin is the easiest tileset in the world to make look like a grey smear: paving, ashlar, mosaic and fallen tile are all weathered pale stone, and they all want the top of the value range together.

So the 8 materials here were placed on a value ladder before any art existed, and the ladder is re-measured from the emitted pixels on every build. It spans 191 luma and no two rungs sit closer than 24.3 apart. That is also the reason the overgrowth is burnt-off summer grass rather than a fresh green.

A bright green would take the top of the ladder and leave the stone nowhere to go — which is how a ruin pack ends up being an overworld pack with rocks on it.
