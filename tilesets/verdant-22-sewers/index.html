A Victorian brick sewer: a barrel vault, granite benching, a channel that runs, cast-iron gratings, a white glazed inspection chamber, silt where the water has dropped its load — and one shaft of daylight coming down through a street grate. 564 of those tiles — 82% of the pack — are autotile transitions, because the transitions are the part that takes the time and the part most packs leave out.

The bright thing in a sewer is the light, not the water A sewer is the easiest tileset in the world to make look like mud: brown brick, brown water, low light, one flat value range across the whole sheet. So the 8 materials here were placed on a value ladder before any art existed, and the ladder is re-measured from the emitted pixels on every build.

No two of them sit closer than twelve luma apart, and the two ends are held by the two things the pack is about: the channel is the darkest thing in it, because depth reads as darkness rather than as blue, and the pool of daylight under the grate is the brightest. That pool carries the hard parallel shadows of the bars above it, at the same bar pitch as the grating material itself.
