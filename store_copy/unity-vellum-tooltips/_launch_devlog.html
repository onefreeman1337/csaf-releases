Vellum is out. It draws a different illuminated card for every item in your Unity project, composed at runtime from the fields your item database already has.

The reason it exists is a number. A tooltip framework gives you positioning, pooling and a hover delay, and then you still supply the art, so a five hundred item game ships eight frames repeated five hundred times. Vellum ships no frames at all. Forty item devices, ten provenance seals, twelve stat glyphs and seven border orders are drawn in C# by a software rasteriser that comes with the source. Forty devices across seven tiers is 280 distinct cards before the seals and stat glyphs multiply it further.

What your data controls. Kind, then Name, then Tags picks the device, matched whole word, so writing Kind = "axe" gets you an axe and writing nothing but "Ashfall Greatsword" still gets you a blade. Rarity picks the palette and the border order, seven orders rather than seven tints. Origin strikes one of ten provenance seals, or none at all when the word is unrecognised, because a seal invented by a hash would be a claim about your item's history that your data never made. ItemLevel sets the device's part count. Worth sets how heavily the card is gilded. Each stat label picks its own glyph.

The package draws no lettering, and that is deliberate. A tooltip's words have to stay selectable, localisable and set in your font. So Vellum draws the sheet, the order, the device, the seal and the glyphs, then publishes normalised rectangles for Title, TypeLine, Stats, Flavour, Device and Seal, and the layout reflows around what the item actually has. An item with two stats and no flavour gives that space back to the illumination.

Determinism. The seed comes from the item's name and kind only, so rebalancing a damage number never redraws the art and your store screenshots do not go stale on the next balance pass.

The editor window sits at Window, CSAF, Vellum and shows any item's card beside a derivation panel that says exactly why it looks that way: which mark was chosen, whether your own word chose it or a hash did, which origin word matched the seal, and every derived scalar. A designer whose sword drew a mace gets an answer instead of a guess.

One number worth publishing honestly: twelve of the forty devices were wrong on the first bake and every one of them passed the compile gate, the coverage floor and the distinctness fingerprint. Five carried an inverted half space, so the axe kept the side the crescent is not on and drew a bare pole. Three were sheared into pinwheels by a radial fold whose shape crossed its own sector boundary. The rest were legible shapes that read as the wrong object. They were found by baking every mark alone onto a review sheet and looking at it. Nothing numeric was ever going to catch them.

Unity 2022.3 or newer. No third party dependencies, no render pipeline dependency, no Resources folder, every script in a named platform constrained assembly definition, and a demo scene you press Play on. Code and graphics are AI generated; every mark is procedural C# and no generative image model was used anywhere in the shipped art path.
