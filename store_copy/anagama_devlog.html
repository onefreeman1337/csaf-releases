ANAGAMA is out, free, and plays in the browser.

It is an incremental about running a Japanese-style climbing kiln. You throw pots, pack them into the chambers up a hillside, light the fire, and then you stop building — from there you have a stoke, a damper and a woodpile, and heat and ash climb the slope with a delay, so you are always firing the past.

The part worth explaining is what comes out. There is no pot image anywhere in the game. Every vessel is drawn from the firing that made it: its form, its glaze, its chamber, which way it faced the flame, and the whole temperature and atmosphere history that actually passed over it. Two identical bowls packed a metre apart come out different, permanently, and the shelf keeps them.

Closing the damper starves the fire of air and pulls the iron in the ash glaze to green; opening it takes the same glaze to amber. It also costs you heat, so holding a reduction means stoking harder — and reducing a cold kiln does nothing at all, which is the one thing a real potter would tell you first.

A ruined firing costs the load and never the shelf.
