A school is not one room repeated. It is a sprung floor hall, a carpeted library, a dining hall, a corridor lined with lockers and display boards, four classrooms including a science lab, and a yard with an all weather pitch and poured wetpour. Nine surfaces, and the interesting part is never the middle of any of them.

It is where they meet. Lino running out to a door sill. Carpet stopping at a threshold strip. Tarmac giving way to wetpour at the edge of the play area. Those joins are what a map is actually made of, and they are the part most packs leave out.

Twelve complete 47 mask sets, and the count is the point

564 of the pack's 783 tiles are transitions. That is 72 percent. Twelve blob sets, each one complete: every inner corner, every outer corner, every edge, every junction and every single. Each set is enumerated over all 256 neighbourhoods rather than drawn from a list somebody typed, so "a transition is missing" is not a state the set can reach.

This matters more than it sounds. A 47 mask set with 44 tiles looks finished in a sheet and breaks in your map, at the one corner you happen to paint on a Tuesday. Completeness is checked mechanically on every build, not eyeballed.

Around them sit 40 fill tiles, 10 materials at 4 seamless variants each, and 42 objects across 147 prop tiles, plus 2 animated sets.

It installs where you actually put it

Worth saying plainly, because for a long time this series got it wrong. Every Godot importer this wing shipped before September resolved its texture paths from the project root. Drop the folder at the top level and it worked. Put it in res://assets/, which is where a real project keeps things, and load() returned null. Not a TileSet with missing textures. No resource at all.

The reason it went unnoticed for months is worth more than the bug: the test harness copied the shipped folder flat into a temporary project root, which made the absolute path resolve by construction. It could ask whether the file was internally consistent. It could not ask whether it worked where a buyer puts it.

This pack is tested by installing it into a subfolder of a blank Godot project, the way you will install it, and counting what comes back.

What is proven, and in what words

The art comes from a procedural pixel engine, no diffusion model and no dataset, which makes the grid, the palette and the absence of anti aliasing true by construction rather than measured afterwards. What is checked mechanically:

  • Godot 4.7.2 built 783 of 783 tiles, from a subfolder. The harness makes Godot count and measure every tile, because a corrupted TileSet loads without error. Counting is the only honest test.
  • Tiled rendered all 16 tilesets through its own tmxrasterizer and every cell was compared to the sheet in your download.
  • 576 of 576 RPG Maker MZ autotile shapes compose byte for byte out of the A2 sheet, using the engine's own autotile table read from the runtime rather than a copy of it.
  • The packed sheets and the loose PNGs were compared to each other, 783 of 783 identical. A pack that ships its art twice can ship two different versions of it.
  • 25 earlier packs, 12,058 tiles, 0 changed. Adding this one to the series is proven not to have moved a single pixel of anything already sold.

What is not claimed: nothing here is advertised as mechanically proven seamless. Every tile's wrap boundary is measured against its own interior and every 4x4 repetition is rendered and reviewed by eye, and those renders ship in the package so you can look for the grid line yourself. The seam test catches hard edge artefacts reliably and the commonest real defect poorly, so it is described as the floor it is.
