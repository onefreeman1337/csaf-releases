Search for a ship tileset and most of what comes back is a sprite: a vessel drawn once, at one angle, at whatever size the artist chose. You can drop it on a map. You cannot stand your player on it, open a hatch, or send them below.

That is a drawing problem answered with a drawing. Verdant 11 answers it as a tiling problem, because a ship is almost entirely edges: deck meeting water, the bulwark standing up on that deck, a hole cut through it, a lid over the hole, a cabin threshold, a tarpaulin simply put down.

Eight complete 47-mask sets, and the count is the point

376 of the pack's 477 tiles are transitions. Eight blob sets, each complete: deck to open sea, hull to open sea, bulwark to deck, hold to deck, grating to deck, cabin to deck, tarpaulin to deck, and deck to granite quay. Each is enumerated over all 256 neighbourhoods rather than drawn from a list somebody typed, so "a transition is missing" is not a state the set can reach.

Seven of the eight are hard edged on purpose. There is no soil on a ship: every surface was cut, planed and fastened, and a jittered edge anywhere would read as damage. The exception is the cloth, which is the exception deliberately.

The brow, deck meeting the granite quay of Verdant 10 Harbour, ships complete inside this pack. You do not need to own Verdant 10 to use it.

What is proven, and in what words

The art comes from a procedural pixel engine, no diffusion model and no dataset, which makes the grid, the palette and the absence of anti aliasing true by construction rather than measured afterwards. What is checked mechanically:

  • Godot 4.7.2 built 477 of 477 tiles. The harness makes Godot count and measure every tile, because a corrupted TileSet loads without error. Counting is the only honest test.
  • Tiled rendered all 14 tilesets through its own tmxrasterizer and every cell was compared to the sheet in your download.
  • 384 of 384 RPG Maker MZ autotile shapes compose byte for byte out of the A2 sheet, using the engine's own autotile table read from the runtime rather than a copy of it.
  • The packed sheets and the loose PNGs were compared to each other, 477 of 477 identical. A pack that ships its art twice can ship two different versions of it.

What is not claimed: nothing here is advertised as mechanically proven seamless. Every tile's wrap boundary is measured against its own interior and every 4x4 repetition is rendered and reviewed by eye, and those renders ship in the package so you can look for the grid line yourself. The seam test catches hard edge artefacts reliably and the commonest real defect poorly, so it is described as the floor it is.

The check that a count could not do

A note from shipping this one, in the spirit of the rest of the series. While uploading the store gallery, one screenshot silently failed and another silently uploaded twice. The image count on the page read five, which was exactly the number expected, so nothing looked wrong.

Counting could never have caught it: five wrong images and five right ones are the same five. The fix was to identify each uploaded image against the file it was supposed to be, at which point the duplicate and the missing one both fell out immediately. When a total can be right for the wrong reasons, check identity instead of quantity.

Every image in the gallery above was matched to its source file before this page went public.
