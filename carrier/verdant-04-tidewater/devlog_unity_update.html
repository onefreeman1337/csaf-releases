Verdant 04 Tidewater now opens in Unity, and the river finally flows the right way. The download has a new folder, importers/Unity/. Copy it into your project's Assets, run one menu command (Tools > CSAF Verdant > Verdant 04 Tidewater > Build Unity Assets), and it builds everything inside your own project. If you already own the pack, download it again to get it. csaf.itch.io/verdant-04-tidewater

What the command makes: the sheets set to pixel exact import, 771 named sprites, 771 Tiles, nine RuleTile brushes (one per transition set: the surf, the reef edge, the river bank, the tideline, the shingle, the marsh, the reeds, the silt and the jetty deck), six AnimatedTiles (the sea swell, the shoal, the marsh sheen, the river running north to south and west to east, and the cascade), and Tile Palettes.

The river is the part we are pleased about. RPG Maker MZ's water slot plays every animation back and forth, which is right for surf washing in and out and wrong for a current, so in MZ the river bank brush stays still. In Unity the river brush runs its three frames as a loop, 0, 1, 2, never backwards. Because this pack never declared a frame time, Unity plays it at MZ's own water speed, half a second a frame, read from the engine's source.

How it was checked: we installed the folder into a subfolder of a blank Unity 6000.5.7f1 project and ran the command twice. Every RuleTile was painted in all 256 possible neighbour arrangements and drew the right tile each time (2,304 of 2,304), every sprite matched the shipped sheets texel for texel, and Unity read every animation back with its frames in order. We read Unity's own animation data rather than recording the editor, and the editor window was not opened. The sample map stays a picture and JSON for now, because it borrows 13 tiles from Verdant 01.

Every tile is drawn by our own procedural pixel art engine, which is AI written software. No image model, no scraped art and no training data.
