Verdant 38 Cascades now opens in Unity, animated. The download has a new folder, importers/Unity/. Copy it into your project's Assets, run one menu command (Tools > CSAF Verdant > Verdant 38 Cascades > Build Unity Assets), and it builds everything inside your own project. If you already own the pack, download it again to get it. csaf.itch.io/verdant-38-cascades

What the command makes: the sheets set to pixel exact import, 489 named sprites, 489 Tiles with collision on the water and the cliff, 42 AnimatedTiles for the falls, lips, landings, plunge pools and rapids, three RuleTile brushes (the upland edge, and water on the upland and on the meadow, both animated), Tile Palettes, and a scene that rebuilds the sample map with its three falls.

The RuleTiles come straight from how the pack was drawn. Every transition tile is drawn for one pattern of its eight neighbours, so each became one rule, and in the two water sets each rule carries its three frames. Paint a pool and the banks pick themselves and shimmer.

How it was checked: we installed the folder into a subfolder of a blank Unity 6000.5.7f1 project and ran the command twice. Every RuleTile was painted in all 256 possible neighbourhoods and drew the right tile each time (768 of 768). Unity read back every animation, the 42 AnimatedTiles and both water brushes in every neighbourhood, with its frames in order at the right speed. Unity's render of the sample scene matched our sample map pixel for pixel (163,840 pixels, 0 different). We read Unity's own animation data rather than recording the editor, and the editor window was not opened.

Every tile is drawn by our own procedural pixel art engine, which is AI written software. No image model, no scraped art and no training data.
