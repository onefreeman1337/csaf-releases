A hot spring hillside at 16x16: 1,137 tiles in one 86 colour palette. Commercial use permitted in unlimited projects, no attribution required; you may not resell the tiles themselves as an asset pack.

Pools held by their terraces, pouring down the slope

Paint a hillside of orange runoff, then two raised levels inside it: cream travertine, and a white sinter crown inside that. Each level is a complete 47 mask autotile and each is lighter than the one below, so the hill brightens as it climbs (94 tiles in the two). A level's whole south edge is the step down, a bright lip over a shaded rock face, and it turns every corner. Lay a drop on the tile below the edge and the step is a full tile tall, with its cast shadow on the ground below. Put a turquoise pool on a level against its south edge (each level has its own pool set, so the rim is that level's ground) and lay dams along the edge under it: the water runs right up to the lip. Wherever it should go over, use a pour in place of a dam: the water spills over the lip in an animated sheet, falls the full step and splashes into the pool you start on the row below. 7 dams, 7 pours and 16 drops in all.

What is in it

  • The terraces: three grounds (the orange runoff apron, the cream level, the white sinter crown), the two raised level sets, a hot pool for each level as a complete 47 shape autotile of shimmering water, open water tiles to break up a big pool, the dams and pours for the water at a terrace edge, and the drops that carry each step down a full tile, with the falling water and its splash.
  • The geothermal features: a two cell wide geyser with an eight frame eruption cycle and geyserite mounds, fields of yellow sulphur crust, grey boiling mud pots with bubbles that swell and burst, fumaroles (grey rock vents) breathing steam, sulphur crystals, and steam columns that rise off any hot water.
  • A bath yard: grey paving stones, a sunken stone bath inside a granite kerb (its own animated water set) fed by a bamboo spout, a wooden bathhouse with a tiled roof and a blank indigo curtain, stone lanterns that flicker, wash stools, buckets, a wooden soaking tub, a towel rack, benches, boulders, stepping stones and a bamboo fence.
  • 32 ground tiles (eight grounds, four variants each) and 893 transition tiles in 11 complete 47 mask sets, 4 of them animated water.
  • 26 objects (counted as pieces: the bamboo fence is three, the boulders three sizes, the mud bubbles and the steam two each), 8 of them animated (geyser, steam rising off hot water, fumarole, mud bubble, small mud bubbles, stone lantern, bamboo spout pouring: 8 pieces, since the steam comes in two heights). Each standing piece carries a contact shadow drawn for its ground.
  • A finished 44x32 sample map, The Steaming Hillside: terraces on three levels running down from the map's corner with a geyser, a sulphur field with mud pots on the upland, and the bath yard, as PNG, JSON and a Tiled .tmx, every tile from this pack.

For a village around the spring, see Verdant 16 East Asian Village. This pack also works on its own: nothing in it needs another pack's files.

Engines

  • RPG Maker MZ: all four hot waters (a pool for each level and the stone bath) on an A1 page as MZ's own animated water (672 of 672 shape frames composed back with MZ's own autotile table), all 11 sets on an A2 page as still brushes, the waters included (528 of 528 shapes), and the grounds, objects and every animation frame on the B, C, D, E pages. MZ animates only its A1 water, so the dams, pours, geyser and steam are stills there, and the drops go on an upper layer. Tested against the table, not inside the MZ editor. Passability is yours to set in the Database.
  • Tiled: one .tsx per sheet with the animations written in, each at its own timing; Tiled 1.12.2's own renderer drew the sample map identical to ours (1,441,792 px, 0 differ), and rendered at 5 instants with the animations advanced, its playback matched our own render of the map exactly.
  • Godot 4: one TileSet, loaded and verified in Godot 4.7.2 (1,137 tiles built). Godot gets the frames as separate tiles, not as animations.
  • Unity: copy importers/Unity/Verdant75Springs/ into your project and run one menu command (Tools > CSAF Verdant > Verdant 75 Springs > Build Unity Assets). It builds 1,137 pixel exact sprites and Tiles, 11 RuleTile brushes (the four waters animated), 39 AnimatedTiles and a scene holding the sample map. Tested in a blank Unity 6000.5.7f1 project: every RuleTile drew the right tile in all 256 neighbour arrangements (2,816 of 2,816), and Unity's render of the scene matched the sample map pixel for pixel. Brushes need the free 2D Tilemap Extras package.

The proofs

Every tile's wrap boundary is measured against its own interior, and every 4x4 repetition is rendered and reviewed. Grid, palette and autotile completeness are machine proven: every tile is exactly 16x16 and every sheet an exact multiple of it, every pixel is inside the 86 colour palette, alpha is strictly on or off, and all 11 sets carry all 47 masks, the water sets in every frame.

AI disclosure

Every tile is generated by our own procedural drawing engine or drawn as pixel rows in code, all of it AI written software. No image model draws any tile, no scraped art and no training dataset were used, and nothing was traced. The cover and every gallery image are made from the pack's own tiles; no image model drew any of them.
