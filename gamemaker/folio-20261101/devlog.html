Folio is out: a book for GameMaker that the player reads and turns by the corner, in pure GML.

You hand it chapters, paragraphs, headings, pictures and drawings of your own, and it sets them on pages: justified lines, no paragraph leaving a single line stranded at the foot or head of a page, headings kept with the lines after them, a drop initial three lines deep opening each chapter, the chapter's name over left-hand pages and the book's title over right-hand ones.

The turn got the most care. The leaf is cut along the fold into flat pieces: the part still lying flat, bands around the curl each shaded by how squarely it faces a lamp above and to the left, and the part that has gone over, which shows the back of the leaf, the next page you will read. It is cut exactly at the quarter turn, so no piece shows both faces, and everything is triangle lists, so it runs on targets that refuse triangle fans. The suite carries 2,000 random drags through it and checks that the spine never lifts and the pieces always add up to the page.

The paper is generated: 48 looks from eight stocks (ivory laid, cream wove, rag white, vellum, parchment, kraft, ledger green and a midnight black written in silver), each ruled six ways, with cut, torn or gilt edges, foxing, frames, page-number devices, headpieces and initials. A frame of text costs 1.8 ms to 2.6 ms at rest and 5.0 ms to 6.6 ms while a leaf turns, on the VM.

A free Windows demo is on the page. Every number here came from real Igor-built Windows VM executables; HTML5, mobile and YYC were not built.
