Your townspeople walk to a station, adopt a real work pose there with a generated tool in hand, and the town visibly changes shift across the day. A smith swings a hammer at an anvil from 06:30; the watch stands at the brazier all night; the baker starts at 04:30 and has finished before most of the street has begun.

Every one of those pictures is drawn from numbers. There is no sprite sheet in this package.

What you get

  • 46 work loops, each a pose that is a continuous function of its phase over 12 motion archetypes - not a table of frames.
  • 46 station props built from 11 shared mass constructors, each returning a back and a front so a worker stands inside a well, a vat or a stall counter instead of behind it.
  • 42 tools, held at the wrist, built from the same constructors.
  • 10 materials, 4 regional themes, 5 builds, 8 hair styles. A theme is one struct: it re-colours every prop, every tool and every garment in the town at once.
  • Clothing with no outfit table. Smock length comes from the stance, sleeves and apron from the trade's hazard, colour from a hash of the work id. Add a work loop and it is dressed.

The idea that makes it cheap to use

lge_where(town, person, hour) solves where somebody is at that hour. It does not integrate movement frame by frame. That buys you:

  • Re-entering a map is exact and free - there is nothing to catch up.
  • Save and load are free: a routine is a few numbers and the clock is yours.
  • Scrubbing the clock backwards works, at any speed, including past midnight.
  • A conversation can pause a person for as long as you like and they resume exactly where they were.

The honest limits, before you buy

Because position is a pure function of time, nothing here is path-dependent. A townsperson cannot avoid a collision, jostle in a doorway or queue, because the answer at time t cannot depend on what happened at t-1. If your game needs NPCs that push past each other, this is the wrong architecture and you should not buy it for that.

The figures are drawn front-on. There is no side or three-quarter view; a figure facing the other way is mirrored, and the lighting is mirrored with it so you do not get two suns in one street.

It draws a lot of primitives. One townsperson at a station is roughly a thousand drawing records. The cache is part of the product, not an afterthought, and the Readme's Performance section gives you the numbers.
