Zarya is a 44 track score for a Slavic folk village in a fantasy game: the izba hearth at dawn, the green and the well, the market square and the haggle, the smithy and the water mill, the wooden church and its bells, the elder's hall, the river at dusk, the birch road and the forest at night, the rye fields and the harvest, the ring dance, the wedding, the midsummer bonfire and the winter carol, the wake, the night watch, and the cues a game fires all day.

One song, every room. Every track states the same 5 note tune, the song the village knows, in its own shape, and every other theme on the record is cut from it. Counted by the harness in the finished MIDI: all 44 tracks state it. This is the grammar of our album Hearthmoot, carried east into a new idiom. The raised fourth, every part. Every track is in one of two modes that raise the fourth.

27 are in Lydian (1, 2, 3, sharp 4, 5, 6, 7) for the village in daylight; 17 are in the mode scale books call Ukrainian Dorian (1, 2, flat 3, sharp 4, 5, 6, flat 7) for the lanes at night, the smithy, the church, the river at dusk, the wake and the watch.
